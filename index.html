    About this film

    An entry for the NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026, "Junior Astronaut Mission Trainer". You play a child whose choices decide which specialist you become on a lunar outpost, and then how your crew rides out a real solar storm by trading power, air and shielding. The child is never shown: the astronaut on screen is your adult future self.

    NASA data, and how it is used

    • The storm is a real one, pulled from NASA's DONKI API.
    • The mission follows the storm's own records: the flare alert, the first measured particles, the cloud from the eruption, and the geomagnetic storm it caused. Each stage of the mission shows that record on screen.
    • The warning time changes the game. If the real particles arrived within an hour, the storm takes shielding before the crew finishes preparing.
    • Why shielding matters comes from NASA's RadLab dosimetry: the same storm read very differently inside the Space Station and in lunar orbit.
    • Food and air use growing times from the Canadian Space Agency's toolkit and NASA's space-crop studies.
    • All of it is archived data. None of it is shown as live.

      How it was made, and where AI was used

      • Picture: every frame is drawn by a program (three.js scenes rendered frame by frame in a browser, then encoded with ffmpeg). The program was written with AI assistance (Claude Fable 5.2, Anthropic). No AI image or video generator was used. Every clip carries a "Made with AI" mark.
      • Voices: AI-generated speech (Kokoro-82M, an open-source text-to-speech model, run on our own computer through mlx-audio).
      • Music and sound effects: synthesized in code (the open-source earshot sound bank).
      • This web player and the text: written with AI assistance. The idea and direction are the team's.
      • Open-source software: three.js, Puppeteer, ffmpeg, Kokoro-82M, mlx-audio, misaki, earshot.
